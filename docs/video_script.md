# 3-Minute Video Script

Record your screen over the executive deck, then 20 seconds of the repo and the Excel model. About 400 words. Thai version below.

| Time | Screen | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Slide 1 | "Hi, I'm Maetasit, a business analyst. Olist, a Brazilian marketplace, grew sales but not customer satisfaction. I analysed 100,000 real orders to find out why, and what to fix first." |
| 0:15 | Slide 2 | "Short answer: delay costs stars, but the delay sits in transit and in a delivery promise that ignores the route. The early-warning system I first planned loses money, so I recommend two cheaper fixes." |
| 0:35 | Slide 3 | "I built a SQL pipeline with 19 data-quality tests, then worked in five streams: diagnosis, customer complaints, prediction, and a business case." |
| 0:50 | Slide 4 | "Every day late costs satisfaction. On time: 4.3 stars. One to three days late: 3.3." |
| 1:05 | Slide 5 | "The surprise: two in three bad reviews come from orders that arrived on time. Orders from several sellers get four times more bad reviews. I'm testing why with a local LLM." |
| 1:25 | Slide 6–7 | "Late orders lose twenty extra days with the carrier, and 72% had a seller who shipped on time. States where the promise has less slack miss it more: the promise isn't calibrated by route." |
| 1:50 | Slide 8 | "A model at payment approval finds 1.7 times more late orders than a simple rule, but only one alert in nine is right, so it's for low-cost actions only." |
| 2:10 | Slide 9 + Excel | "In the business case the early warning loses forty thousand reais in year one; it needs about thirteen times today's volume. So I'd park it." |
| 2:30 | Slide 10 | "My recommendation: calibrate the promise by route and add a seller handover SLA, and prove it with a six-week A/B test of about 8,400 orders." |
| 2:50 | GitHub | "Everything is reproducible on GitHub. Thanks for watching." |
